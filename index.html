About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Michigan. My research lies at the intersection of computer systems, artificial intelligence for science, and laboratory automation. I am particularly interested in designing the software infrastructure that connects AI agents to physical experiments, enabling autonomous laboratories to operate reliably and efficiently.

My work draws on research in automated battery electrolyte characterization, scientific instrumentation, and AI-driven experimental planning. My long-term goal is to make scientific laboratories more programmable, accessible, and reproducible, so researchers and AI systems can move from hypotheses to experimental evidence more effectively.

Research

My current projects focus on building the systems foundations of autonomous scientific discovery.

  • Experiment-as-Code Laboratories. I explore declarative abstractions for laboratory experiments, separating scientific intent from device-specific control logic. This approach enables validation, resource management, scheduling, and reproducible execution across heterogeneous instruments. [Paper]
  • AI Agents for Autonomous Experimentation. I develop agent-based workflows that translate natural-language scientific objectives into experimental plans, verify feasibility, coordinate instruments, monitor execution, and use measurement results to inform subsequent experiments. [Code]
  • Self-Driving Laboratories for Battery Electrolytes. I have contributed to ElyteOS, a Python-based platform for experiment control and data management, and SALSA, a modular system for automated salt-solubility measurements. Together, these tools support more flexible and data-driven electrolyte research. [ElyteOS] [SALSA]
